Slide 1:
What a neural network is
In brief:
• A model made of layers of simple computing units connected to one another.
• Each layer takes what it receives and transforms it into a form that is more useful to the next layer.
Key idea:
• Several layers chained together can learn increasingly complex relationships.

Slide 2:
How the model learns
One iteration, step by step:
1. It receives an input and produces a prediction.
2. It compares the prediction with the correct answer and measures the error.
3. It slightly adjusts its internal settings so it makes a smaller error next time.
Remember:
• By repeating this over many examples, the model gradually approaches the right answer.

Slide 3:
Why layers help
What each level does:
• Early layers detect small details.
• Middle layers combine them into larger patterns.
• Final layers reach general, abstract ideas.
Trade-off:
• More layers mean more power, but also a greater risk of memorizing instead of understanding.

Slide 4:
A useful analogy
Think of a team:
• Each person solves a small part and passes it onward.
• No one sees everything, but together they solve a difficult task.
Lesson:
• The power does not come from one brilliant unit, but from many simple units connected well.
